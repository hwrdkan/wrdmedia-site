Why coating buyers need help picturing and trusting

A coated floor is a visual choice made once, and the homeowner usually cannot tell a good prep job from a bad one until the coating fails. They compare colors in a small sample and quotes with very different scopes. The contractor who shows the result on the buyer's own floor and explains prep plainly stands apart.

Epoxy vs polyaspartic: what installers say, and what is verified

Garage coating installers generally say epoxy can yellow in sunlight and can suffer hot tire pickup, while polyaspartic is marketed as UV-stable, faster curing, and more tolerant of hot tires, usually at a higher price. Most of those statements come from companies selling the coatings, so treat them as vendor claims, not independent test results.

The point installers agree on is preparation: surface prep drives how well either coating performs, and one installer puts it at about 80 percent of success. A contractor who explains prep and moisture testing clearly is more trustworthy than one who only shows a glossy finish.

Concrete and clay: the North Texas angle

Much of North Texas sits on expansive clay, often called Blackland Prairie soil. Sources describe it as shrink-swell clay that cracks when dry and expands when wet, and slabs, driveways, and patios on it commonly crack and move unevenly. Drainage matters as much as the slab design. Contractors who explain this honestly help homeowners understand why a crack appears and what can and cannot be coated over.

Many of these pages are written by foundation-repair companies, so for any specific slab the guidance is to follow local code and an engineer's recommendation, and to ask about subgrade preparation, moisture control, control joints, and drainage.

Moisture testing and what to ask a coating contractor

Common industry practice is to test a slab for moisture vapor before coating, using a calcium chloride test (ASTM F1869) or in-situ relative humidity probes (ASTM F2170), and to check the product's moisture vapor limit on the manufacturer's data sheet. Questions buyers should ask:

  • How do you prep the surface, and what tool do you use?
  • How do you test the slab for moisture, and what result do you require?
  • What is the product's data sheet limit, and what is the warranty?
  • How do you handle cracks and control joints before coating?
  • How long until I can drive on it?
