Plano is a large, established market

Plano's 2020 census base was 285,465 residents, and the Census Bureau's July 2023 estimate was 290,190, a change of about 1.7 percent. That is a very different picture from fast-growing Prosper: Plano is largely built out, mostly in Collin County, with areas such as Legacy West, Downtown Plano, and Willow Bend each drawing different homeowners.

For contractors, a built-out city means the work is remodels, replacements, and upgrades on existing homes, and the competition is long established. Standing out depends less on being new and more on being clearer: clear answers, a complete Google listing, and recent real work.

What Plano publishes: patio covers

Plano's residential patio cover checklist is specific. According to the city's document:

  • Two copies of a complete set of drawings are required for plan review.
  • The site plan should show the patio cover area shaded, plus the existing house and setback dimensions from the property lines.
  • The added patio cover, together with existing lot coverage, cannot exceed the maximum lot coverage allowed.
  • Patio covers are not allowed in any required side, front, or rear yard setback.
  • A permit left inactive for 180 days is treated as abandoned, though it can be renewed for another 180 days for a fee.

Third-party guides add that covered patios with structural posts generally need footing and framing inspections, but those are not the city's own statements. A contractor who walks a homeowner through the lot-coverage and setback limits before the quote earns trust and avoids a surprise.

What Plano publishes: fences

The city's residential fence checklist says:

  • A site plan showing the fence location is required, and masonry fences or fences with masonry columns also need footing drawings.
  • In the required front yard, fences may be no taller than 40 inches and must be at least 50 percent open.
  • Corner lots must comply with sight visibility clips.
  • Masonry fences, brick columns, and retaining walls over 4 feet must be designed and sealed by a Texas registered engineer.
  • A footing inspection, if applicable, and a final fence inspection are required.
  • Repairs covering no more than 25 percent of a fence's area in a 12-month period do not need a permit.
  • A new fence installed within three feet of an existing fence on the same lot requires removing the old one.

Fences around pools have special requirements. We did not find an official Plano pool page, so check the city's swimming pool information directly. Plano Building Inspections lists (972) 941-7143.

According to the city's fence checklist, fences in the required front yard may be no taller than 40 inches and must be at least 50 percent open. Confirm with the city.

Do I need a permit to repair a fence in Plano?

The city's checklist says repairs covering no more than 25 percent of a fence's area in a 12-month period do not need a permit. Larger changes do. Confirm with the city.
