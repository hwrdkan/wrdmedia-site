The full answer

Dallas-Fort Worth is many local markets, not one. Howard Kan acts as a fractional CMO for trade and home-service businesses across the metroplex, helping decide which cities to win first and what to say in each.

What makes Dallas-Fort Worth a distinct marketing market?

Rules, HOAs, and weather change from city to city across the metroplex. Frisco, McKinney, Prosper, Plano, and Allen each publish different guidance, and North Texas adds a hail season and heat that affect roofs, fences, louvers, and turf.

A business that serves several cities has a strategic question a single-city business does not: where to concentrate. Trying to rank in every city at once can spread the effort too thin.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in Dallas-Fort Worth?

  • Choosing priority cities based on where your best jobs, crews, and margins already are.
  • Service areas, honestly. Listing the areas you really serve, and not creating fake locations.
  • One hub, supporting local pages. A clear metroplex page supported by genuine city pages where you have verified local information and real work.
  • Hail and heat planning. Searches for hail help tend to rise after storms, so readiness before the season matters.
  • One system for follow-up so every city's leads get the same fast reply.

What do Dallas-Fort Worth homeowners ask, and how should you answer?

Across DFW, homeowners ask the same kinds of questions in different words and with different rules behind them: permits, HOA approval, how much it costs, and who to trust after a storm. The right answer often depends on the city.

The metroplex page keeps the comparison: Dallas-Fort Worth: how rules change city to city. A fractional CMO decides which cities and questions come first.

What would the first 90 days focus on for Dallas-Fort Worth?

Month one maps where your best jobs and margins are by city. Month two picks the one or two cities to win first and writes the roadmap for them. Month three builds the hub and the first genuine city pages, and puts one follow-up system across every city.

How does the engagement work for a Dallas-Fort Worth business?

The engagement is the same everywhere: a free 20-minute strategy call, a written 90-day plan, then weekly execution and monthly reporting. Advisor ($4,999 a month) is senior direction for the team you already have; Embedded ($9,999 a month) adds live working sessions four times a week, continuous hands-on improvements, and the Ascend program run for you. Both have a 90-day initial term, you keep everything we build, and Howard Kan takes three clients at a time. Details are on the fractional CMO page.

We do not promise rankings or lead counts for Dallas-Fort Worth or anywhere else. We commit to documented work each month and an honest report.

Mistakes to avoid in Dallas-Fort Worth

  • Spreading thin across the whole metroplex before winning one or two cities.
  • Creating pages for cities you do not serve.
  • Letting each city's leads follow a different, slower process.
