The full answer

Pergolas are a visual, high-ticket purchase that buyers struggle to picture, so they collect quotes and compare on price. Howard Kan acts as a fractional CMO for pergola and outdoor living contractors in DFW, shaping the plan that gets better-qualified calls.

What is the marketing problem in pergola and outdoor living?

The homeowner often cannot picture a pergola. They do not know whether a 12 by 16 will fit, what louvers change, or whether the number they hear is fair, so they browse photos, collect three quotes, and compare on price, often the easiest thing to compare.

For the contractor the result can be estimator afternoons spent measuring yards for buyers who were never going to buy, and slow replies to serious ones. The fix is clarity before the call, and a plan that makes it happen.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in pergola and outdoor living?

  • Quote clarity. Explain what drives price: material, size, attached versus freestanding, louvers, lighting, and finishes.
  • Permit and HOA answers. Frisco requires a permit for accessory structures such as patio covers, gazebos, and arbors and requires registered contractors; Prosper requires permits for pergolas, patio covers, arbors, and sheds. A site that states them accurately earns trust.
  • Qualifying before the estimate. A design tool or good intake questions save estimator time.
  • Google profile and AI answers. Buyers ask who builds the best pergola in their city.
  • Vendor and installer oversight. Make sure marketing promises match what the crew can deliver.

What do pergola and outdoor living buyers ask before they call?

Buyers ask whether they need a permit, whether a louvered aluminum pergola needs engineered plans, how much a pergola costs, whether the HOA will allow it, and who builds them locally.

Mistakes to avoid in pergola and outdoor living marketing

  • Competing on the lowest quote instead of the clearest one.
  • Ignoring permit and HOA questions that every buyer has.
  • Measuring estimates booked instead of estimates that turn into jobs.
