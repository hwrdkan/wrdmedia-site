A very high-ticket, very visual purchase

A pool is among the largest outdoor spending decisions a household makes. The buyer wants to see shape, size, finish, and decking in their own yard, and they want to know the real cost before they invite three builders to bid. The more a builder can show and explain before the meeting, the better the meeting goes.

What changes the price of a pool

Pool prices vary more than almost any other home project:

  • Pool type: gunite, fiberglass, or vinyl, and whether it is in-ground or aboveground.
  • Size, depth, and shape.
  • Decking, coping, and landscaping around it.
  • Spa, waterfall, lighting, and heating features.
  • Excavation and access to the yard.
  • Barrier fencing, permits, and electrical and plumbing work.

We do not publish a universal price table. Prices change, they vary by lot and design, and a table of averages would be a guess. The reliable number is a written quote for the specific project. A design tool lets a buyer shape the pool and see how each choice changes the range.

Permits and barrier rules in the cities we serve

McKinney requires a barrier at least four feet tall around any pool, spa, or water feature 24 inches deep or more, with self-closing, self-latching gates and doors. Its pool permit packet asks for a pool plan, a fence detail, a survey, and utility approval, notes the pool cannot encroach on easements, and says HOA approval may be needed.

The City of Dallas requires a permit for pools, with an exception for a small prefabricated pool that sits entirely above grade, holds no more than 5,000 gallons, and is under 24 inches deep. Electrical and plumbing work tied to a pool needs its own permits. Frisco and Prosper have their own rules, so confirm with the city.

Check before you build. Permit rules, fees, and HOA requirements change and vary by property. This page summarizes what cities have published. Confirm with your city and your HOA before work starts.

Common questions

How much does an inground pool cost in Dallas?

It depends on pool type, size, decking, features, excavation, barrier fencing, and permits. We do not publish a universal price. Get itemized quotes from established local builders.

What barrier is required around a pool in McKinney?

A barrier at least four feet tall around any pool, spa, or water feature 24 inches deep or more, with self-closing, self-latching gates and doors.

Does a pool need a permit in Dallas?

Yes, except a small prefabricated pool that sits entirely above grade, holds no more than 5,000 gallons, and is under 24 inches deep. Electrical and plumbing work needs separate permits.
