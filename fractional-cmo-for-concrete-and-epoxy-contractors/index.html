What is the marketing problem in concrete and epoxy coating?

The homeowner compares colors in a small sample and quotes with very different scopes, and cannot judge prep quality. The contractor who explains prep plainly and shows the result on the buyer's own floor stands apart.

North Texas adds a local wrinkle: much of the region sits on expansive clay, which can crack and move slabs. Honest explanation of that builds credibility.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in concrete and epoxy coating?

  • Prep as the selling point. Explain surface prep, the tool used, and why it matters.
  • Moisture testing. Common practice is to test a slab for moisture vapor before coating, using calcium chloride tests (ASTM F1869) or in-situ relative humidity probes (ASTM F2170), and to check the product's data sheet. Buyers should ask about it.
  • Honest product claims. Statements about epoxy and polyaspartic often come from companies selling the coatings, so treat them as vendor claims, not independent test results.
  • The clay conversation. Explain cracking and movement honestly.
  • Visualizing the floor. Show the finish on the buyer's own floor.

What do concrete and epoxy coating buyers ask before they call?

Buyers ask how long a coating lasts, how epoxy and polyaspartic differ, how the slab is prepped and tested, what to do about cracks, and what the warranty covers.

Mistakes to avoid in concrete and epoxy coating marketing

  • Repeating vendor marketing claims as if they were test results.
  • Selling color and finish while saying nothing about prep and moisture.
  • Promising crack-free results on a clay-soil slab.
