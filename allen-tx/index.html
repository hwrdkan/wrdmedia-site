Allen at a glance

Allen is in central Collin County, about 25 miles north of Dallas along US 75, with the State Highway 121 corridor on its northern edge. The 2020 census counted 104,627 residents, and the Census Bureau's July 2023 estimate was 111,620. Growth here is steadier than in Prosper or Frisco, so the market is more about serving and upgrading established homes than a stream of brand-new ones.

Local landmarks give a sense of the area homeowners spend time in: Watters Creek at Montgomery Farm, a mixed-use shopping and dining district, the city-owned Credit Union of Texas Event Center (formerly the Allen Event Center), and the Twin Creeks community around the Golf Club at Twin Creeks. A contractor with real jobs near these places has something genuine to say about them.

Allen permit rules: what we found and what we did not

We looked for the City of Allen's own published permit pages for patio covers, pergolas, fences, and pools, and did not find them. Third-party permit guides exist, but they conflict: one says a covered patio attached to the home or a freestanding one over 200 square feet generally needs a permit, another puts the threshold at 300 square feet for covered decks. A partial city fee schedule lists a flat fee for fences and above-ground pools, but it may be out of date.

We would rather say that plainly than guess. For an Allen project, contact the city's Development Services department before building, and ask whether the structure is attached, how big it is, what setbacks apply, and whether a site plan and engineered drawings are required. Contractors who tell homeowners exactly that, and who handle the permit, stand out.

Questions to ask any contractor in Allen

  • Are you registered or permitted with the City of Allen, and who pulls the permit?
  • Is the structure attached or freestanding, and what does the city require for that size?
  • What setbacks and easements apply on my lot, and who confirms them?
  • Does my HOA need to approve this, and will you prepare the submittal?
  • What is included in the price: footings, electrical, finish, haul-away, and cleanup?
  • Can I see recent finished jobs near me, and can I speak with a past customer?

These questions work in any city, and a contractor who answers them in writing before the quote earns the call.

HOAs and architectural approval

Many Allen neighborhoods have homeowners associations with architectural review. For pergolas, patio covers, fences, turf, and pools, HOA approval is often the slowest step, so the best contractors start it early and explain it plainly. Your own declaration and architectural guidelines control, so read them before you order materials, and keep a dated copy of whatever you submit.
