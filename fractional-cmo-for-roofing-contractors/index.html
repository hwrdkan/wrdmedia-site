The full answer

Roofing is an urgent, expensive purchase made in a market where storm chasers appear after every hailstorm. Howard Kan acts as a fractional CMO for roofing contractors in DFW, preparing the business before the season so it is ready when searches rise.

What is the marketing problem in roofing?

A roof is a big, urgent purchase, and the homeowner may not know what a good bid looks like. After a storm, door-to-door crews and out-of-town contractors arrive, and many homeowners have heard a horror story. They look for a roofer who looks local, explains the process, and shows the result.

Searches for hail help tend to rise in the days after a storm. If your profile, pages, and business details are in order beforehand you are in the running. If you start afterward you are late.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in roofing?

  • Season readiness. Complete the Google profile, answer the specific hail and insurance questions, and match business details everywhere before the storm.
  • Licensing and insurance facts. Industry sources say Texas does not issue a state roofing license, and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation does not license roofers. Some cities require registration or permits. Explaining this builds credibility.
  • Insurance clarity. Wind and hail deductibles are often a percentage of insured value, which homeowners need to understand. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles cost more, and some insurers discount for them while others do not, so buyers should ask their carrier.
  • Look local and prove it. Real local jobs, reviews, and named people.
  • Lead speed after a storm. The first useful reply wins.

What do roofing buyers ask before they call?

Buyers ask whether their roof has hail damage, whether a Class 4 roof is worth it in North Texas, whether roofers need a license in Texas, and who replaces roofs after hail.

Mistakes to avoid in roofing marketing

  • Starting after the storm instead of before it.
  • Looking like a storm chaser: no local proof and no named people.
  • Staying vague on insurance, which is the homeowner's biggest worry.
