Pergola cost in Frisco, McKinney, and Prosper

Published 2026 guides put installed pergolas at roughly $25 to $85 per square foot nationally, from about $25 to $40 for pressure-treated wood to $60 to over $100 for motorized louvered aluminum. A typical backyard pergola in North Texas lands between about $8,000 and $22,000, and permits, engineered plans, and HOA approval can add time and cost.

For whole projects, one guide estimates a typical 12 by 12 louvered pergola at about $8,000 low, $12,000 average, and $22,000 high, and a basic aluminum fixed-louver pergola of 150 to 200 square feet at $8,000 to $15,000. Larger motorized systems can reach $20,000 to $40,000. One guide notes regional labor and site prep can add 10 to 25 percent.

What changes the quote

  • Material. Wood, cedar, aluminum, or louvered aluminum.
  • Size and attachment. Attached covers are treated differently from freestanding structures.
  • Louvers and motors. Adjustable louvers and motors add cost.
  • Wind and heat. North Texas weather affects structural and material choices.
  • Electrical. Lights, fans, heaters, and outlets need permits and add cost.
  • Footings and flatwork. The concrete beneath it.
  • Permits and engineered plans. Required in some cities, as below.

Permit rules in Frisco, McKinney, and Prosper

Frisco. The city's building inspections FAQ says accessory structures, including patio covers, gazebos, and arbors, need a building permit before placement or construction and must meet setbacks. Contractors who work in Frisco must be registered before a permit is issued. The city lists (972) 292-5301.

Prosper. The Town's homeowner guidance requires a permit for patio covers, pergolas, arbors, and sheds, attached or freestanding, and says all accessory buildings need a permit regardless of size. Attached covers over 400 square feet need plans sealed by a registered design professional, and metal or other structures outside the International Residential Code need a plan set sealed by a Texas-licensed architect, engineer, or landscape architect. The Town lists (972) 346-3502.

McKinney. We did not find McKinney's specific pergola thresholds published. One third-party guide says covered patio structures typically need a permit, especially if attached or large, but that is not the city's own statement. Contact McKinney Development Services before you build.

If you are in an HOA, you will usually also need architectural approval. Start that early, because it is often the slowest step.

Check before you build. Prices, permit rules, and laws change and vary by property. This guide summarizes published sources and is not legal or financial advice. Confirm with your city, your HOA, your insurer, and local contractors.

Questions to ask a pergola contractor

  • Who pulls the permit, and are you registered in my city?
  • Is this attached or freestanding, and what does the city require for that?
  • Do I need engineered or sealed plans for this material and size?
  • What wind and heat ratings does this system have?
  • What is included: footings, electrical, finish, and cleanup?
  • Can I see a 3D design of exactly what you are proposing?

Common questions

How much does a pergola cost in Texas?

Published guides put installed pergolas at about $25 to $85 per square foot nationally, with a typical backyard project in the $8,000 to $22,000 range. Get local quotes, since North Texas labor, permits, and site prep vary.

Do I need a permit for a pergola in Frisco?

Frisco's building inspections FAQ says accessory structures including patio covers, gazebos, and arbors require a building permit before construction. Confirm with the city at (972) 292-5301.

Do I need a permit for a pergola in Prosper?

Yes. The Town requires a permit for pergolas, patio covers, and arbors, attached or freestanding, and sealed plans for larger attached covers and for metal structures outside the residential code.

Does an aluminum or louvered pergola need engineered plans in Prosper?

According to the Town's published handout, metal or other structures outside the International Residential Code need a plan set sealed by a Texas-licensed architect, engineer, or landscape architect. Confirm with the Town.

Do I need HOA approval for a pergola?

If your neighborhood has an HOA, you usually need architectural approval before building. Check your declaration and apply early.
