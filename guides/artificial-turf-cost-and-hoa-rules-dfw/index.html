Artificial turf: what drives the cost, and the HOA rules in Dallas-Fort Worth

Why turf quotes differ

Two turf quotes can look far apart because one includes a deep compacted base, drainage work, and haul-away, and the other does not. Ask for an itemized quote that lists base depth and material, drainage, tear-out, the turf product with specs, edging and anchoring, and warranty. We do not publish a universal price per square foot, because averages hide these differences.

What changes the price

  • Tear-out of existing grass and haul-away.
  • Grading and drainage work.
  • Base depth and material. A proper base is most of the quality.
  • Turf grade and warranty length.
  • Edging, anchoring, and wind resistance.
  • Permits where the city requires them.

What Texas law says about HOAs and turf

Texas Property Code Section 202.007 prevents a property owners' association from including or enforcing a provision that prohibits or restricts an owner's use of drought-resistant landscaping or water-conserving natural turf. An association may still require a plan for review, may not unreasonably deny it, and may restrict the type of turf used for new planting to encourage water-conserving turf.

The statute refers to natural turf. We found no source extending its protections to synthetic grass, so an HOA may be able to prohibit or limit artificial turf under its covenants and guidelines. A 2025 bill proposed changes to the plan-review requirement, and we could not confirm its outcome, so read the current statute text. If you are in a dispute, talk to a Texas attorney.

Check before you build. Prices, permit rules, and laws change and vary by property. This guide summarizes published sources and is not legal or financial advice. Confirm with your city, your HOA, your insurer, and local contractors.

How to get HOA approval for turf

  1. Read your declaration and architectural guidelines for turf, landscaping, and visible surfaces.
  2. Prepare a plan: area, photos, product sample, and a render of the finished look.
  3. Submit before ordering materials and keep a dated copy.
  4. Respond to questions quickly, and ask for any denial in writing with the reasons.

Texas heat, pets, and drainage: what to ask

Artificial turf can get noticeably hotter than natural grass in direct sun, so ask installers about heat-reflective or cooling products and look at samples in the sun. Ask how pet waste and odor are handled and what infill is used. Ask how the base drains, since clay soils common in parts of North Texas hold water. A proper compacted base is where quality shows, so ask for the base depth and material in writing.

Questions to ask turf installers

  • What is the base depth and material, and what is the compaction method?
  • How do you handle drainage on my lot?
  • What is the pile height, face weight, and warranty for this product?
  • How are the edges and seams secured against wind?
  • Who handles HOA submittals, and can I see a render of the finished look?
  • Is tear-out and haul-away included in the price?

Common questions

What changes the cost of artificial turf in Dallas?

It depends on tear-out, grading and drainage, base depth and material, turf grade, edging, and the size of the area. Get itemized written quotes and compare what each includes.

Can my HOA ban artificial turf in Texas?

Section 202.007 protects drought-resistant landscaping and water-conserving natural turf. It is written about natural turf, so an HOA may be able to restrict artificial turf under its covenants. Check your declaration and consult a Texas attorney.

Can an HOA require approval for landscaping?

Yes. The statute lets an association require a plan for review, but it may not unreasonably deny approval of protected landscaping.

Does artificial turf get hot in Texas?

It can. Heat depends on the turf, infill, and sun exposure, so ask installers about heat-reflective products and look at samples in sun.

What should be in a turf quote?

Area, tear-out, base depth and material, drainage, turf product with specs, edging and anchoring, warranty, and who handles permits or HOA paperwork.
