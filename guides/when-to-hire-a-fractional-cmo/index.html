When to hire a fractional CMO

Hire a fractional CMO when your business already sells, marketing matters to growth, and nobody owns the plan. The usual signs are scattered effort, an agency you cannot evaluate, leads that go cold, and an owner with no time to run marketing.

Eight signs you are ready

  • No one owns marketing. It happens in bursts when someone has time.
  • You cannot tell if your agency is earning its fee. The reports are activity, not results.
  • Leads go cold. New inquiries wait hours or days for a reply, and quotes are never followed up.
  • You are busy but not growing. More effort, same revenue.
  • Your online presence is thin or inconsistent. Your Google profile, website, and listings disagree with each other.
  • Competitors show up when you search, and you do not. That includes AI answers, not just Google.
  • You have a team or vendors but no direction for them.
  • You are about to spend on something big such as a website rebuild, a tool, or a large ad budget, and want a second senior opinion first.

Five signs you are not ready

  • You have not made your first sales yet. Sales comes before marketing leadership.
  • You want a single task such as a logo or one landing page.
  • You cannot give access to your accounts, numbers, and decisions. A CMO cannot lead blind.
  • You expect guaranteed rankings, leads, or revenue. No honest practitioner can promise those.
  • You cannot commit to a few months. Strategy needs time to show up in results.

What should be true before you start?

  • You know roughly what a customer is worth and what you want to grow.
  • Someone can approve work quickly.
  • You are willing to share real numbers.

What does it look like in a trade business?

A roofing company that wins most work after hailstorms but has no plan for the quiet months. A pergola builder whose estimator spends afternoons measuring yards for buyers who never buy. A fence or pool business with good referrals and a website that does not show the work. In each case the first job is the same: find the one or two changes that matter most, then get them done. A quick self-check

Answer yes or no to each question. Three or more yes answers suggest you are ready to talk to someone.

  • If I asked who owns marketing here, would there be a pause?
  • Do I know which marketing activity actually produced my last five jobs?
  • Does a new inquiry get a reply in under an hour, every time?
  • Could I explain, in one sentence, what my agency or marketer did last month and what it earned?
  • Is there a written plan for the next 90 days?

For the first four, a no is the warning sign. For the last one, a no means there is nothing to hold the work to, which is what a fractional CMO provides.

What to prepare before the first call

  • A rough idea of your average job value and how many jobs you want to add.
  • Access to your website, Google Business Profile, and any ad accounts.
  • A list of the vendors, freelancers, and staff who touch marketing.
  • What you have tried and what happened, including the failures.

You do not need a polished brief. An honest account of what is and is not working is more useful than a deck.

Common questions

How big should my business be?

There is no fixed size. It fits established businesses that already sell and can invest in marketing. A common range is a small team with steady work and room to grow.

Should I hire an agency first?

Only if you already know the strategy. Without direction, an agency fills the gap with what it sells.

Is it too early for my business?

If you have not sold yet, probably yes. Focus on sales first.

What if I already have a marketing person?

A fractional CMO can lead and coach them. Many hires struggle for lack of direction.

How quickly will I see results?

Fixes to follow-up speed and offers can show up in weeks. Authority on Google and in AI answers builds over months.
