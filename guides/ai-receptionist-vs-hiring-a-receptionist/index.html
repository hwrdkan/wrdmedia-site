AI receptionist vs hiring a receptionist: what each really costs

An AI receptionist is a phone and text assistant that answers calls, qualifies the caller, and books appointments at any hour. A human receptionist brings judgment and warmth but covers only staffed hours and carries salary, benefits, and onboarding costs. For most trade businesses the right answer is both: the AI for coverage and a person for complex calls.

What an AI receptionist costs

AI receptionist pricing ranges from self-serve plans to custom builds, and most published comparisons are written by vendors, so they lean favorable. Self-serve plans are cheap because they are generic. A custom build tied to your calendar, your qualifying questions, and your CRM costs more up front.

WRD Media provides AI receptionists as part of the Architect plan. See AI phone assistants.

What a human receptionist costs

A receptionist's cost is more than the hourly wage. Add employer taxes, benefits, paid time off, recruiting, onboarding, and the cost of calls going to voicemail during lunch, sick days, and after hours. A part-time hire costs less but covers fewer hours. For exact local wage data, see the Bureau of Labor Statistics occupational wage data for your area.

Where each one wins

  • AI wins on nights, weekends, holidays, and busy moments, on consistency, and on cost.
  • A human wins on empathy, sensitive situations, complicated questions, and relationships.
  • A hybrid wins for most trades: the AI answers and books, and hands off to a person when a call needs one.

How to decide

Count how many calls you miss, when, and what a booked job is worth. If you miss calls while on jobs and at night, an AI receptionist pays for itself quickly. If your calls are mostly complex or emotional, put a human first. Our missed calls guide explains which statistics to trust.

Break-even math you can do yourself

Here is an illustration with made-up round numbers, so replace them with your own. Say you miss 20 calls a month while working, and 1 in 10 of those would have become a $6,000 job. That is two jobs, or $12,000 of revenue, a month at risk. If an AI receptionist catches even half, it covers its monthly fee many times over. If you miss two calls a month and your average job is small, it will not. The point is to count your own calls, which our missed calls guide shows how to do in a week.

What to ask any AI receptionist vendor

  • Can I hear real call recordings or read transcripts?
  • Does it connect to my calendar and CRM, or only send me a message?
  • What happens when it does not know the answer?
  • Who sets up and maintains it after launch?
  • What are all the fees: setup, monthly, per minute, and overage?

Can an AI receptionist replace a human?

For routine calls and after-hours coverage, yes. For complex or sensitive calls, a human is still better, so a handoff matters.

How do I know if I need one?

Count how many calls you miss in a month, how many come outside working hours, and what a booked job is worth. If the numbers are small, you may not need one.
