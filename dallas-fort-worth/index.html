How the rules change from city to city

One of the most useful things a contractor's website can do is explain local rules clearly. Here is what the cities we have researched publish. Always confirm with the city before building, since rules change and depend on the property.

Summary of published city rules. Not legal advice. Verify with each city.
CityWhat the city publishes
FriscoAccessory structures, including patio covers, gazebos, and arbors, need a building permit before construction and must meet setbacks. Contractors must be registered before a permit is issued.
McKinneyPools, spas, and water features 24 inches deep or more need a barrier at least four feet tall with self-closing, self-latching gates. The pool permit packet asks for a survey, a fence detail, and a utility sign-off, and notes HOA approval may be needed.
ProsperPergolas, patio covers, arbors, and sheds need a permit, attached or freestanding. Attached covers over 400 square feet need sealed plans, and metal structures outside the residential code need plans sealed by a Texas-licensed design professional. Fence work over 16 linear feet needs a permit.
DallasPools need a permit, except a small prefabricated pool that sits entirely above grade, holds no more than 5,000 gallons, and is under 24 inches deep. Electrical and plumbing work tied to a pool needs its own permits.
PlanoThe residential patio cover checklist asks for two sets of drawings and a site plan with setbacks, caps total lot coverage, and does not allow patio covers in required yard setbacks. Front-yard fences may be no taller than 40 inches and at least 50 percent open.
AllenWe could not find the city's own published rules for patio covers, fences, or pools, and third-party guides disagree on thresholds. Confirm with Allen Development Services.

Hail, HOAs, and heat shape every project here

North Texas has a hail season, and roofs, fences, pergola louvers, and turf all take a beating. Homeowners search for hail-related help in clusters after a storm, and the businesses that already have clear, specific pages are the ones that get the call. See our guide to roof replacement and hail claims.

Most newer neighborhoods in the metroplex have an HOA with architectural review. For pergolas, fences, pools, and turf, HOA approval is often the slowest step in the project. Texas has laws about how associations must handle some approvals, but the details are specific. Contractors who explain the HOA process earn trust. Our turf cost and HOA guide is an example of that kind of page.

Summer heat also matters: wind and heat ratings for shade structures, and heat-reflective turf. These are the details buyers ask AI about.

Common questions

Which cities in Dallas-Fort Worth do you serve?

Our deepest local pages cover Frisco, McKinney, and Prosper. We also work with businesses in Plano, Allen, Dallas, The Colony, Little Elm, Celina, and elsewhere in the metroplex, plus remotely with businesses in other states.

Do permit rules differ between DFW cities?

Yes. Each city publishes its own rules. For example, Prosper publishes specific permit requirements for pergolas and patio covers, McKinney publishes pool barrier rules, and Frisco requires contractors to be registered before permits are issued. Always confirm with the city.

Why does hail matter for marketing?

After a hail storm, homeowners search heavily for roofing, fence, and exterior repair. Businesses that already have clear, specific answers and a strong Google listing are far more likely to be found in that surge.
