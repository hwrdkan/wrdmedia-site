Why roofing buyers need help picturing and trusting

A roof is a big, urgent, expensive purchase, and the homeowner usually does not know what a good bid looks like. They are also wary: after a storm, door-to-door crews and out-of-town contractors pour in, and many homeowners have heard a horror story. They look for a roofer who looks local, explains the process clearly, and shows the result.

Two things win that buyer: a clear, honest answer to the questions they search, and the ability to see shingle style and color on their own house before committing. The first is content. The second is a visualizer.

Licensing and insurance: the facts a good roofer explains

Texas does not issue a state roofing license, according to several industry sources, and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, which licenses electricians and HVAC contractors, does not license roofers. Some cities require contractors to register or pull permits, and requirements change by city. The Roofing Contractors Association of Texas offers a voluntary credential, but it is not a government license.

Insurance is another area where homeowners need straight answers. Reporting in the Dallas Morning News notes that only a licensed public insurance adjuster may work with an insurer on a homeowner's behalf. Texas also restricts contractors from waiving or absorbing a homeowner's insurance deductible, so check with the Texas Department of Insurance for current rules. A roofer who explains these rules plainly, and links the sources, stands out from the storm chasers.

Check before you build. Permit rules, fees, and HOA requirements change and vary by property. This page summarizes what cities have published. Confirm with your city and your HOA before work starts.

What changes the price of a roof

Roof prices vary by size, pitch, material, tear-off, decking repair, and the shingle line. Class 4 impact-resistant shingles cost more than standard shingles, and some insurers discount premiums for them while others do not, so ask your carrier. Insurance deductibles for wind and hail are often a percentage of the home's insured value, which homeowners need to check in their own policy.

We do not publish a universal price table. Prices change, they vary by lot and design, and a table of averages would be a guess. The reliable number is a written quote for the specific project. See our guide to roof replacement and hail in DFW.

Common questions

Do roofers need a license in Texas?

Several industry sources say Texas has no state roofing license, and the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation does not license roofers. Some cities require registration or permits. A voluntary credential from the Roofing Contractors Association of Texas exists but is not a government license.

Reporting in the Dallas Morning News notes that only a licensed public insurance adjuster may work with an insurer on behalf of a homeowner. Homeowners should check the Texas Department of Insurance for current rules.
