The full answer

A fence looks simple, so buyers assume quotes should match, then see wide gaps and do not know why. Howard Kan acts as a fractional CMO for fence contractors in DFW, building the plan that makes quotes understandable and calls better qualified.

What is the marketing problem in fencing?

The gaps between fence quotes often come from material, height, post type, gates, and what is included. A contractor who explains those differences wins the buyer who is tired of guessing.

That takes answers to the questions people search and a way to let them pick options. It also takes someone owning the plan so it actually gets published.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in fencing?

  • Price-driver content. Material (pine, cedar, metal, or masonry), height and style, wood or steel posts, gates, staining or sealing, removal of an old fence, slope and access.
  • Permit and rule answers. The Town of Prosper requires a fence permit unless replacing under 16 linear feet with like materials; McKinney requires pool barrier fences at least four feet tall with self-closing, self-latching gates. Others vary, so buyers should check the city.
  • Qualifying calls. A simple option picker or intake questions filter tire-kickers.
  • Local proof. Finished fences in named neighborhoods.
  • Quote follow-up. Many quotes die with no second contact.

What do fencing buyers ask before they call?

Buyers ask how much a fence costs, whether they need a permit, what fence a pool needs, whether the HOA allows a style, and how long it takes.

Mistakes to avoid in fencing marketing

  • Publishing a universal price table that is wrong for most lots.
