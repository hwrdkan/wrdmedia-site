What is the marketing problem in pool and spa?

The buyer wants to see shape, size, finish, and decking in their own yard, and wants to know the real cost before inviting three builders to bid. The more a builder can show and explain before the meeting, the better the meeting goes.

This is a long-consideration sale, so the marketing job is staying useful and visible through weeks or months, not just catching a click.

What would a fractional CMO look at first in pool and spa?

  • Rules explained. McKinney requires a barrier at least four feet tall around any pool, spa, or water feature 24 inches deep or more, with self-closing, self-latching gates and doors; its pool permit packet asks for a pool plan, fence detail, survey, and utility approval. Buyers appreciate a builder who explains the process.
  • Price drivers. Pool type, size and depth, decking and coping, spa and features, excavation and access, barrier fencing, permits, and utilities.
  • Nurture. A follow-up rhythm for buyers who take months to decide.
  • Seeing it in their yard. Visuals that show shape and size.
  • Reviews and project stories. Trust is the main currency.

What do pool and spa buyers ask before they call?

Buyers ask what a pool costs, what barrier or fence is required, how long it takes, whether the HOA must approve, and who is reliable.

Mistakes to avoid in pool and spa marketing

  • Treating a months-long decision like a one-click purchase.
  • Skipping the permit and barrier explanation buyers worry about.
